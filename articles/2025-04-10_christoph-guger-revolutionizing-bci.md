## The Birth of g.tec

Christoph Guger grew up in an entrepreneurial environment, inspired by his parents’ business. From an early age, he knew he would start his own company but was torn between two fields: building wind power plants or developing BCI systems.

While both had their appeal, BCI’s potential to revolutionize healthcare, neuroscience, and human-computer interaction stood out. Unlike wind power, shaped by politics and regulations, BCI is driven purely by innovation and its ability to improve lives.

This passion led to the founding of g.tec, dedicated to advancing neurotechnology for brain research, rehabilitation, and communication.

Looking back, it was the right choice—each day is spent pushing the boundaries of science, collaborating with top researchers, and witnessing the life-changing impact of BCI innovations.

Real-Time Data Access and Device Limitations 25 years ago, BCI technology began at g.tec, with only a few research labs exploring its potential. At the time, BCIs were primarily used to help locked-in patients communicate by mentally controlling a cursor on a screen—an approach that was simple yet groundbreaking.

One of the biggest challenges was the lack of real-time access to EEG data. Existing devices were locked, preventing researchers from processing brain signals live or developing interactive applications. EEG systems were also large and bulky, limiting BCIs to lab environments.

To solve these issues, g.tec developed its own EEG hardware and software, starting with g.USBamp— the first biosignal amplifier to provide real-time signal access with an open API.

This breakthrough gave researchers the freedom to process brain signals live, accelerating innovation. Over time, EEG systems became smaller, portable, and user-friendly, expanding BCI applications into medicine, communication, and gaming.

Today, BCI technology is widely recognized, but raising awareness and proving its reliability remain essential for its continued growth.

## Advancing Neurotechnology

The future of neurotechnology is advancing rapidly, with g.tec at the forefront. BCIs are transforming medical treatments, neurorehabilitation, and everyday life.

A major breakthrough is cortiQ 2.0, a system revolutionizing neurosurgery, especially for children. It maps the eloquent cortex, helping neurosurgeons identify critical brain regions for movement, speech, and sensory processing. In glioma surgery, cortiQ mapping improves tumor removal while preserving healthy tissue, leading to longer patient survival.

Beyond neurosurgery, BCI technology is driving progress in neurorehabilitation, assistive technology, and AI-driven brain augmentation. High-resolution EEG systems like the 1014-channel g.Pangolin are unlocking new possibilities in brain mapping and neuroscience research. With continuous innovation, g.tec is shaping the future of neurotechnology to improve lives and expand human potential.
