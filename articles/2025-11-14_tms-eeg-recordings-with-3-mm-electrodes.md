TMS-EEG is one of the most powerful combined neurostimulation and neuroimaging approaches for studying brain function. By synchronizing transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) with high-resolution electroencephalography (EEG), researchers can measure cortical excitability, network interactions, and immediate responses to targeted stimulation. High-quality TMS-EEG recordings depend on hardware specifically optimized to handle strong electromagnetic pulses while maintaining clean EEG signals—with electrode geometry playing a central role in recording fidelity.

This article presents a technical overview of modern TMS-EEG methodologies, with a focus on ultra-flat 3 mm electrodes engineered to maximize coil–scalp proximity, reduce artifacts, and ensure reliable data acquisition across a broad frequency range.

Understanding the Fundamentals of TMS-EEG Recordings

**TMS-EEG integrates two modalities:** TMS delivers brief, high-intensity magnetic pulses to cortical regions.

EEG records electrical potentials from neural populations responding to the pulse.

Together, TMS-EEG recordings enable high-temporal-resolution measurements of cortical reactivity.

Simultaneous Stimulation and Recording Challenges

During a TMS pulse, the coil generates:

## Rapidly changing magnetic fields

Large induced voltages in adjacent conductors

## Mechanical vibrations

## Saturation effects in nearby electronics

These factors create artifacts that must be minimized by design rather than simply filtered out. Electrode geometry, materials, and integrated electronics significantly influence how quickly the EEG signal recovers after stimulation.

## Importance of Electrode Geometry in TMS-EEG Research

Electrode thickness directly affects coil placement and artifact management.

## How Electrode Thickness Influences Artifact Formation

A thick electrode increases the coil–scalp distance, which:

## Weakens the magnetic field reaching the cortex

Forces researchers to increase stimulation intensity

## Leads to larger EEG artifacts

## Reduces focality of stimulation

To achieve optimal TMS-EEG recordings, the electrode must be as thin as possible—without sacrificing durability or signal quality.

## Ultra-Flat Electrode Technology: Benefits in TMS-EEG Recordings

The introduction of 3 mm ultra-flat electrodes represents a major advancement in TMS-compatible EEG technology. As the flattest TMS electrode available, this design allows the coil to be positioned extremely close to the scalp, enhancing both stimulation precision and EEG quality.

Impact on TMS Pulse Strength and Crosstalk

By reducing coil–scalp distance:

Less stimulation power is needed

## Pulse artifacts become smaller

Electric field distribution becomes more uniform

## Coil stability improves during repetitive TMS

These benefits are especially valuable in protocols involving high-frequency pulses or TMS-evoked potential mapping.

## Active Electrode Systems for TMS-EEG

Active electrodes integrate miniature amplifiers directly at the scalp. This reduces cable-induced noise and improves overall signal fidelity.

**Benefits include:** Strong EEG signals even with higher impedance levels

Fast recovery (<10 ms) following TMS pulses

## Reduced environmental noise sensitivity

## High-Impedance Operation and Recovery Times

Because the amplifier boosts the signal before it travels through the cable, active electrodes maintain clean output even when impedances fluctuate—an advantage in multi-channel setups.

## Passive Electrode Systems for TMS-EEG

Passive electrodes contain no internal electronics, making them ideal for certain TMS applications.

**Advantages:** Extremely fast post-pulse recovery

## Minimal risk of amplifier saturation

Cleaner capture of early TMS-evoked potentials

## Achieving Low Impedance for High-Fidelity Data

Passive electrodes require abrasive gel to reach 1–5 kΩ, ensuring:

Stable signal quality

## Minimal drift

## High consistency across sessions
